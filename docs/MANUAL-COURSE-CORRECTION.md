# Fly Apollo 13's manual course correction

Press **F12** after Kerwin reports **0.2 mmHg** in the CO₂ adapter chapter. Both
adapters keep running and the cabin stays cold. The intervening coast is
condensed. MCC-5 was the first return-leg correction after the trans-Earth
injection burn. The descent engine fired from **105:18:28 to 105:18:42 GET**.
[NASA mission report, table 6.4-I](https://www.apollojournals.org/alsj/a13/A13_MissionReport.pdf)

1. Use **F** to find the highlighted cockpit control. Set up the backup
   attitude controls and open the commander's COAS. Press **Enter** to move on
   once the checklist is set.
2. Use **Left/Right** for yaw and **Z/X** for roll. Center Earth left to right
   and level the terminator with the COAS Y–Y line, daylight on top. Press
   **Enter** once steady.
3. Set the AOT azimuth to **F** (detent 2) with the cursor at zero. Use **F** to
   return to the requested sight and **V** to switch views. Use **Up/Down** to
   place the Sun at the top of the AOT cursor. Confirm with **Enter**.
4. Copy the PAD, then follow the engine checks. Set the gimbal to OFF,
   select manual throttle and leave it at its 10% minimum, and arm the descent
   engine. **Ctrl+click** releases the engine-arm center lock.
5. Hold **Space** for four-jet +X ullage. At **10.0 s**, press **Enter** while
   still holding Space to press ENGINE START. Release Space after ignition.
6. Steer with Earth in the sight. At **14.0 s** on the burn timer press
   **Backspace** for ENGINE STOP. Both START and STOP can also be operated
   at their cockpit controls. After Houston's trim call, pulse **Space**
   until DEDA 470 reads **+7.6 ft/s**, then release it.

**R** repeats the latest radio call and **M** toggles radio audio. The checklist
keeps its captions. **Shift+F12** restarts the maneuver while in the chapter.
After the successful trim, **F12** continues to
[Odyssey power-up](ODYSSEY-POWER-UP.md).
**F5 / F9** saves and restores progress, including a burn in flight. Held inputs
release on restore, so a saved ullage needs a fresh hold.

The original transmissions cover Duke's attitude setup, Brand's two optical
references and Lousma's PAD, clearance, gimbal check and trim. Excerpts are
timed to player actions, so the two optical calls play in sighting order. NASA
recorded the audio. Apollo 13 in Real Time credits the restoration to Ben Feist
and Jeremy Cooper and lists its work under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0. The manifest records
the tape channel, excerpts, offsets and transcript links.
[Flight journal](https://apollojournals.org/afj/ap13fj/19day5-themanualcoursecorrection.html) · [Restored archive](https://apolloinrealtime.org/13/) · [Audio manifest](../assets/audio/mcc5/manifest.json)

The chapter combines the crew's stations into one desktop control scheme. The
COAS uses the cabin's modeled sight and Earth globe. The AOT is a reconstructed
field, and the event timer and preselected DEDA address 470 use scenario
readouts. The attitude, engine impulse and trim are a local physics model.
[Duke's backup-control read-up](https://apollojournals.org/afj/ap13fj/17day5-thumpandsnowflakes.html)
